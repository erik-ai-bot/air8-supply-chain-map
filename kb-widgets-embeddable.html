▸Why Netherlands is the real risk, not Ross
Three things move Action and Toi-Toys together:
Consumer cycle. Same Benelux value consumer — one recession, both contract.
EUR/USD FX. Both pay in EUR. 10% USD strengthening = direct margin hit for BanaToys with no offset.
EU Toy Safety Directive. NL is one of the most active enforcers at EU border — random sampling rate is above EU average.

Ross, by contrast, is A-rated, US-domiciled, and less than 1% of BanaToys's book. The credit risk in this relationship runs the other way.

▸The one thing that breaks EU compliance — phthalates
Phthalates are the #1 EU通关 rejection trigger for injection-moulded plastic toys. EU limits DEHP, DBP, BBP to ≤0.1% each — a hard limit. US CPSC has no equivalent; it uses voluntary guidance + Prop 65 CA exposure thresholds.

Same pellets, same mould, two specs. One non-compliant resin batch flags the EU SKU at border while the US SKU sails through. The risk lives upstream of BanaToys — at the PP/AS resin supplier. Factory QC cert is meaningless if the input material is non-compliant.

Fix cost: ~$200–400 per SKU for a full REACH/phthalates test. One test covers the whole HS 9503 pretend-play line. One factory visit to audit the resin supplier list closes the material-layer gap permanently.
▸What the compliance rings show — and what they don't
"Not sighted" means just that — we have not seen the document in BanaToys's file. It does not mean the document does not exist.
The factory certifies more than appears in our record; each such item is a question to ask, not a finding against BanaToys.

The most important open questions for this supplier:
① Has BanaToys been tested at the resin input level for phthalates, or only at the finished-goods level?
② Does the CE mark on file cover the specific HS 9503 SKU shipped to Action/Toi-Toys, or a broader product range?
③ Is there a BSCI or amfori BSCI audit on file? (Factory social audit — common NL buyer requirement)
